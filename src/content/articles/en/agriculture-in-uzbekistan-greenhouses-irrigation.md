Agriculture in Uzbekistan is moving from volume to efficiency, and the main driver of that shift is water. For suppliers of irrigation, greenhouses, seeds, fertilisers, crop protection and machinery, it opens real demand, but only for those who understand how a farm decides, when it buys and what it needs to see first. This guide is for suppliers entering the market and for farms looking for a technology partner.

## Agriculture in Uzbekistan: why water sets the agenda

Most cropland in the country depends on irrigation, and water is the resource that limits every other decision on a farm. Furrow irrigation loses much of the water before it reaches the roots, so any talk about yield or new plantings quickly becomes a talk about water.

The state actively promotes water-saving technologies, and drip and sprinkler systems are part of public support programmes. Conditions change, so confirm the current rules with the agriculture ministry, water management authorities and partner banks before building an offer around them.

For a supplier of drip irrigation in Uzbekistan, this means three things:

- Payback in saved water and higher yield matters more to the farmer than the equipment price per hectare.
- Design and installation are part of the product, because a farm buys a working system, not a pallet of pipes.
- Filtration, fertigation and water quality need local answers, since water sources and soils differ from one district to the next.

## Greenhouses, orchards and export: where new investment goes

### Greenhouses and year-round vegetables

The greenhouse business in Uzbekistan grows on a simple logic: sunny winters make it possible to supply tomatoes, cucumbers and greens in the months when the open-field harvest is over and prices are higher. Investors range from family farms with a film tunnel to agro-holdings building glass or polycarbonate complexes.

Their questions are predictable: winter heating costs, summer cooling and shading, substrates, climate control and a realistic turnkey timeline. A supplier with a working reference in a similar climate gets a serious hearing.

### Orchards and intensive horticulture

Horticulture is shifting toward intensive orchards with dense planting, trellis systems and drip irrigation from the first year. The purchase is a bundle: planting material, trellis, irrigation, nets and agronomic support in the first seasons.

### Export of fruit and vegetables

Fresh and dried fruit, vegetables and melons go to neighbouring countries and further abroad, and export buyers set the standard: calibration, residue limits for crop protection products, packaging, cold storage and traceability. This pulls demand toward sorting lines, cold stores, certified inputs and laboratory testing. Exporters should check the importing country's phytosanitary requirements with the relevant inspection authority before each season.

## Machinery and service: what farms check first

Agricultural machinery in Uzbekistan is bought after the farmer has seen it work and knows who will repair it. The first question at a stand is rarely about horsepower; it is about service:

- Where the nearest service point is and how fast a technician arrives during the season.
- Which spare parts are kept in stock inside the country rather than in a warehouse abroad.
- Whether operators are trained on delivery and whether manuals exist in Uzbek or Russian.
- Whether the machine suits local field sizes, soils and crops, from cotton and wheat to vegetables and orchards.

A brand without a local dealer and parts stock competes on price alone and usually loses; a brand with a service network competes on uptime.

## How to demo and sell agricultural technology

Farmers trust what they can see in their own conditions. A sales plan that works usually follows these steps:

1. Set up a demo plot with a respected local farm or cluster and document the whole season: water use, yield and input costs.
2. Bring the results, photos and the host farmer's contact to every meeting, because a neighbour's result sells better than a brochure.
3. Package design, installation, training and after-sales service into one offer with a clear total price.
4. Offer a financing route by working with leasing companies and banks that already lend to agriculture, and prepare the documents they need from you as the supplier.
5. Keep agronomic support after the sale, at least through the first season, so that the system or input is used correctly.

For seeds, fertilisers and crop protection products, registration comes before any sale. National authorities set the rules for registering varieties and agrochemicals, so confirm the procedure and timeline with the relevant ministry or a local distributor well in advance.

## The buying calendar: timing matters more than discounts

Agricultural purchasing follows the season, and a supplier who arrives late waits a full year. The typical rhythm looks like this:

- Autumn after harvest: farms review results, plan new plantings and start discussing irrigation projects and greenhouse construction.
- Winter: budgets, financing applications and supplier selection, when quotes and specifications are compared side by side.
- Late winter and early spring: final orders for machinery, seeds, fertilisers and crop protection before field work begins.
- Spring and summer: installation, deliveries, service visits and demo days in the field.

Financing approvals take weeks, so an offer made in March for a system needed in April is often too late. Meet the buyer at least one season before you expect the order.

## FAQ

### Is drip irrigation in Uzbekistan supported by the state?

Yes, the state promotes water-saving technologies and drip systems are part of support programmes, but the conditions change. Confirm the current rules with the agriculture ministry, the water management authorities or partner banks before quoting them to a farm.

### When should a supplier start talking to farms?

At least one season ahead. Autumn and winter are for planning and financing, and orders for spring are usually finalised by early March.

### Do I need a local partner to sell agricultural machinery?

In practice, yes. Farms buy where service and spare parts are available locally, so a dealer or service partner in the country is often the deciding factor.

### What should I bring to an agriculture exhibition in Uzbekistan?

Results from a demo plot, a working sample or fragment of your system, a price that includes installation and service, a financing option and registration or certification documents for your products.
