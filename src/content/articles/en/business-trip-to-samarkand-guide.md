A business trip to Samarkand for a trade show is short, dense and easy to get wrong in the small details: a sold-out train, a hotel on the wrong side of town, a card that does not work in a taxi. This guide is for exhibitors, buyers and delegations travelling to a show at SOF EXPO Samarkand: what to book and when, how to get there, and how to spend three days so the trip pays for itself.

## Planning a business trip to Samarkand: 3–4 weeks before the show

Most of the work happens a month out. Start from the show dates and work backwards: FOODERA EXPO runs 27–29 October 2026 and BUILD PRO EXPO 9–11 November 2026, and exhibitors usually need to arrive the day before opening to check the stand.

- Check entry rules for every traveller's passport: many nationalities enter Uzbekistan visa-free, others apply for the official e-visa, and the rules change, so confirm with the nearest Uzbek embassy or the official e-visa portal.
- Book the long leg first: an international flight to Samarkand or to Tashkent, then the train.
- Buy train tickets 2–3 weeks ahead; seats on the fast trains around show dates go early.
- Reserve the hotel once dates are fixed and ask about the participant discount.
- Send the list of meetings you want to the show team so that slots can be booked before you fly.
- Prepare printed materials in Russian and, ideally, Uzbek, plus English for international visitors.

## How to get to Samarkand: air, train and road

There are three realistic routes, and the right one depends on where you start.

### By air

Samarkand International Airport (SKD) takes flights from Tashkent, Istanbul, Dubai and Moscow. If there is no convenient direct flight from your city, fly into Tashkent (TAS) and continue by train. The exhibition centre is 16 km from the airport, about 20 minutes by car, and the route does not cross the city centre.

### Tashkent to Samarkand train

The Afrosiyob and Sharq high-speed trains link Tashkent and Samarkand in about 1 hour 40 minutes, which is usually faster door to door than a connecting flight. Business class costs noticeably more than a standard seat, so decide in advance who in the team needs it. Samarkand station is 23 km from the venue, about 35 minutes by car; for groups the venue team can arrange a coach at the platform.

### By road

The M39 highway from Tashkent takes 4–5 hours, and Bukhara is about 3 hours away. Driving makes sense if you carry samples or travel as a group. Parking at the centre is free and has spaces for coaches and trucks.

## Samarkand hotels for business: choose by distance to the venue

The venue sits in the Dzhambay district on the outbound side of Samarkand, about 12 km from the Registan. That leaves two sensible options and two rules that apply to both.

- A hotel in the historic centre puts you 20 minutes from the hall and walking distance from restaurants for evening meetings.
- A hotel on the airport or venue side shortens the morning drive, which matters for exhibitors on build day.
- The partner hotel Reikartz gives SOF EXPO participants 15% off when you mention the exhibition code at booking.
- Whatever you choose, ask for reliable Wi-Fi, a quiet room and an early breakfast, because show days start before the doors open.

A common mistake is booking by photos of the old city and discovering a long commute. Check the route to the venue on a map before paying.

## On the ground: taxis, money, SIM and internet

Getting around is simple if you prepare before landing.

- Ride-hailing apps are widely used in Uzbek cities; install one at home and order cars to and from the venue rather than negotiating at the kerb.
- The national currency is the soum. International cards work in many hotels and larger restaurants, but keep some cash for small cafés, markets and street taxis, and exchange money at banks or official exchange points.
- A local SIM card can be bought at the airport; bring your passport, as registration is required.
- Free Wi-Fi covers the whole exhibition centre, and exhibitors can order a wired connection to the stand.
- Samarkand is on UTC+5; set reminders for calls with head office accordingly.

## Business etiquette and meeting culture

Business in Uzbekistan runs on personal relationships, and a trade show is where they start. A few habits help.

- Uzbek is the state language and Russian is widely used in business, while English is less common outside international companies, so bring an interpreter if your team does not speak Russian.
- Greet with a handshake, exchange business cards and expect tea before the commercial part of a meeting.
- The decision often rests with the most senior person in the room; address your offer to them, but keep the specialists involved.
- Do not push for a signature in the first meeting. Agree the next step and a date instead.
- Follow up quickly: many partners prefer a short message in Telegram to a long e-mail.
- Dress in business attire for meetings and modestly for visits to mosques and mausoleums.

## A 3-day itinerary around a show

This plan covers three days on the ground for someone arriving the day before opening; exhibitors staying for the whole show repeat day 2.

1. Day 1, arrival: morning train or flight, hotel check-in, afternoon at the venue to check the stand, collect badges and walk the route between your target exhibitors.
2. Day 1, evening: dinner near the Registan, the square framed by three madrasahs, which is especially striking after dark.
3. Day 2, the show: meetings in the morning before the main flow, the business programme at midday, lunch at one of the two on-site cafés with 350 seats in total, and follow-up notes written the same evening.
4. Day 2, evening: a working dinner with a partner in the old city, or a walk to Gur-e-Amir, the mausoleum of Amir Timur.
5. Day 3, sites and second meetings: visit a distributor's warehouse or a client's site in the morning, return to the show for second-round talks, and take the evening train or flight home.

If you can stay an extra half-day, add the Shah-i-Zinda necropolis or the Siab bazaar. Delegations of several companies should also read our separate guide on group visits.

## FAQ

### How long does the Tashkent to Samarkand train take?

The high-speed Afrosiyob and Sharq trains take about 1 hour 40 minutes; buy tickets 2–3 weeks before the show.

### How far is the exhibition centre from the airport and the city?

SOF EXPO Samarkand is 16 km from the airport, 23 km from the railway station and about 12 km from the Registan, each 20–35 minutes by car.

### Do I need a visa for Uzbekistan?

Many nationalities enter visa-free and others use the official e-visa; check the current rules for your passport with an Uzbek embassy before booking.

### Can the venue arrange a transfer?

Yes, transfers for delegations from the airport or the station can be ordered through the venue contacts page.
