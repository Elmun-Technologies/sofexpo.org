The construction market in Uzbekistan is busy and broad, driven by new housing, private homes, infrastructure and a steady stream of hotel and tourism projects. For manufacturers and distributors of building materials, facades, roofing, windows, finishing, tools and engineering systems, the opportunity is real, but the market rewards suppliers who are present locally rather than those who only send price lists. This guide explains where demand comes from, who decides, and what a foreign supplier needs before the first order.

## Where demand in the construction market in Uzbekistan comes from

Demand is spread across several segments, and each of them buys differently:

- Multi-storey housing: developers build residential complexes in Tashkent, Samarkand and regional centres, buying large volumes after long negotiations.
- Private homes: families build and renovate their own houses, often in stages over several years, and buy through building markets, dealers and DIY stores.
- Infrastructure and public buildings: roads, utilities, schools and hospitals follow tender procedures with strict documentation requirements.
- Tourism and hospitality: Samarkand's role as a destination supports hotels, guesthouses and restaurants, as well as sensitive projects near the historic centre, where finishing quality and design matter.
- Commercial and industrial sites: warehouses, shopping centres and production facilities need roofing, sandwich panels, flooring and engineering systems.

For a supplier, the first decision is which segment to target first. A facade system for a hotel developer and a tile collection for a private homeowner need different partners, prices and stock.

## Who decides what is bought

In construction, the party that pays is often not the party that chooses. Map the decision chain before you plan any meetings.

### Developers and contractors

Developers set the budget and the quality level of a project, and general contractors choose specific materials within it, often under pressure to cut costs. Contractors value reliable delivery, technical support on site and payment terms.

### Architects and designers

Architects and interior designers write materials into the specification. If a product is not in the project at the design stage, it usually has to fight a cheaper substitute later. What they need from you is samples, technical data sheets, CAD or BIM files and reference projects.

### Dealers, wholesalers and DIY retail

Dealers and wholesalers cover the regions and the private home segment, while retail chains and DIY stores reach homeowners directly. They decide on margin, turnover and how easily a product can be explained to the end customer.

## What building materials suppliers need before the first order

Interest at a meeting turns into orders only when the basics are in place. A practical checklist for building materials suppliers entering the country:

1. Local stock: a warehouse in the country or a distributor holding inventory, because contractors rarely wait weeks for an imported delivery.
2. Certificates: conformity documents recognised in Uzbekistan for each product line, prepared before sales start rather than after the first inquiry.
3. Dealer network: one strong partner per region works better than many weak ones, with clear territories and a consistent price policy.
4. Samples and documentation: physical samples, catalogues and technical sheets in Russian and ideally Uzbek, together with installation instructions.
5. Price in local currency: a price list in soums with delivery terms, so buyers can compare offers without converting.
6. Training: installation courses for contractors and dealer staff, because poor installation damages a brand faster than any competitor.

Certification and import requirements change, so confirm the current rules with the national standards and certification body and with a customs broker before you ship goods.

## Building materials in Uzbekistan: climate and local norms

Samarkand and most of Uzbekistan have a dry continental climate with hot summers, cold winters and large daily temperature swings. Materials must cope with strong sun, high surface temperatures and dust. This matters for facades, roofing, sealants, paints, window profiles and insulation, and for the energy performance of buildings that need cooling in summer and heating in winter.

The region is also seismically active, and local building norms set requirements for structures, materials and fastening systems. A supplier of structural products, facade substructures, glazing or heavy cladding should check which national building codes apply to its products and confirm compliance with local design institutes or certification bodies before offering them to projects.

Test results in hot conditions and a clear explanation of how a system behaves in a seismic zone build more trust than general claims about quality.

## FAQ

### Do I need local stock to sell building materials in Uzbekistan?

In most segments, yes. Contractors and dealers expect quick delivery, so a local warehouse or a distributor holding stock is usually a condition for regular orders.

### Who should I meet first: developers, architects or dealers?

It depends on the product. Specification products such as facades and engineering systems start with architects and developers, while finishing materials and tools for private homes start with dealers and retail.

### Which building norms apply to my products?

National building codes and certification rules apply, including seismic requirements for structural elements. Check the current requirements with the national standards body and local design institutes.
