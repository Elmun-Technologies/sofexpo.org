Exhibiting in Uzbekistan as a foreign company is mostly a logistics project: the stand itself is the easy part, while visas, sample shipping and the team's travel decide whether you open on time. This guide gives a foreign exhibitor an end-to-end operational plan, from the first booking to the last box leaving the hall. The document pack for products, certification and labelling is a separate topic, covered in our guide to exhibitor documents, and is only touched on here.

## Exhibiting in Uzbekistan: the timeline for a foreign team

Work backwards from the opening day. A realistic schedule for a team flying in from abroad looks like this:

1. Around 16 weeks before: book the stand, confirm the hall or outdoor location and name a project owner on your side.
2. Around 12 weeks before: request an invitation letter if your team needs one and choose a freight forwarder.
3. Around 8 weeks before: approve the stand design, send the sample list to the forwarder and book interpreters.
4. Around 6 weeks before: dispatch samples, book flights and hotels, and confirm power and water needs with the venue.
5. Final 3 weeks: send print-ready graphics, check customs status, brief the team and arrange airport pickups.

The exact dates depend on your country, your products and the transport route, so treat these as a starting point and adjust with your forwarder. The same sequence works for any trade show in Uzbekistan for foreign companies, not only in Samarkand.

## Invitation letter, visas and entry

Many nationalities can enter Uzbekistan without a visa, and many others use the electronic visa. The rules change, so check your team's passports against the official e-visa portal of Uzbekistan and with the Uzbek embassy or consulate in your country before you book anything.

If your visa application needs an invitation, ask the organiser for the letter early. Expect to provide the full name, passport details, nationality and dates of stay for each person. Apply as soon as you have it, and build a buffer in case one team member is delayed.

Also check that every passport is valid well beyond your return date and that travel insurance covers business trips.

## Shipping exhibition samples to Uzbekistan

This is where most foreign exhibitors lose time. Decide early which goods come back and which stay.

### Temporary import or samples consumed at the show

Exhibits that will be shown and then taken home, such as machinery, equipment or display units, usually travel under a temporary import procedure. Goods that will be used up at the show, such as tasting portions, giveaways and printed materials, are treated differently and may be subject to duties and taxes. Goods you plan to leave behind or sell after the show need a regular import clearance. Split these categories clearly on the packing list to avoid delays.

### Working with a freight forwarder and a customs broker

Use a forwarder with experience of exhibition cargo into Uzbekistan and a local customs broker who will clear the goods. Give them a detailed list with descriptions, quantities, values, weights and HS codes, and say which items return. Ask them which temporary import procedure applies to your goods and what guarantee, if any, is required. Do not assume that a procedure used in other countries is accepted.

### Timelines and delivery to the venue

Road, rail and air routes have very different transit times, and customs clearance adds its own days. Agree with your forwarder on a delivery date to the venue before build-up starts, not on the opening day. The venue offers unloading, moves inside the hall and guarded storage, so confirm the delivery window with the organiser and label every box with your stand number.

## Stand build-up, interpreters and on-site team

At SOF EXPO Samarkand a shell scheme stand needs about 30 days of lead time, while a custom stand needs its design and load calculation about 45 days before build-up. Print-ready graphics are due around two to three weeks before the show. If you bring a builder from abroad, check their tools, materials and work permits with the organiser; it is often simpler to order the build locally and send only graphics and exhibits.

Plan the stand team around languages. Russian is widely used in business and Uzbek is the state language, so a team that speaks only English or Chinese needs help. The venue can arrange interpreting in Russian, English, Uzbek and Turkish, plus simultaneous interpreting for a conference session. Book interpreters early and brief them on your products, prices and terms.

A practical team for a mid-sized stand:

- One decision maker who can agree prices and sign.
- One technical specialist who can answer product questions.
- One interpreter per person who talks to visitors, or a bilingual local sales manager.
- One person responsible for leads, logistics and the stand itself.

## Payments and contracts in a foreign currency

Foreign exhibitors usually pay for the stand and services by international bank transfer according to the invoice and contract. Check the currency, bank fees and the payment deadline in advance, and allow time for the transfer to arrive.

For deals with local buyers, contracts in US dollars or euros are common in foreign trade, but currency control and banking rules apply on the Uzbek side. Agree the currency, payment terms, delivery terms and governing law in writing, and have the contract checked by a lawyer who knows local practice. Selling goods at the stand is a separate question that depends on a local entity or partner; the documents guide explains it.

## Travel to Samarkand for the team

Samarkand airport takes flights from Tashkent, Istanbul, Dubai and Moscow, and the venue is about 16 km from it. Another route is to fly to Tashkent and take the high-speed train, which takes about 1 hour 40 minutes. Book hotels early for show dates and arrange daily transfers to the venue, especially for the build-up days.

Arrive at least one day before build-up. It gives you time to collect badges, check the delivered cargo and fix small problems before the first visitors walk in.

## FAQ

### How early should a foreign company book a stand?

Three to four months ahead is a comfortable window for a foreign team. It leaves time for visas, sample shipping and stand design without paying for rush services.

### Do we need a visa to exhibit in Uzbekistan?

It depends on your nationality. Many visitors are visa-free or use the e-visa; check the official e-visa portal and the Uzbek embassy, and ask the organiser for an invitation letter if needed.

### Can we bring our own stand builder?

Often yes, but agree it with the organiser in advance, including materials, safety rules and access times. A local builder avoids shipping the structure and already knows the venue rules.

### What happens to samples after the show?

Temporarily imported exhibits must leave the country or be cleared for import. Plan the return shipment, or a sale to a local partner, before the show ends.
