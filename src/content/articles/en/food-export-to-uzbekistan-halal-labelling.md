Food export to Uzbekistan rarely fails at the tasting; it fails between the handshake and the first truck crossing the border. Buyers want the product, but they also want a conformity document, a label in Uzbek, a halal certificate where the category calls for one and a price that leaves room for the distributor. This guide takes a foreign producer through those checks in the order a buyer raises them.

## What buyers in Uzbekistan check before a first order

Retail chains, wholesalers and HoReCa operators buy differently, but their first questions overlap. Expect to be asked for the following.

- A conformity document for each SKU you intend to ship, not only for the hero product.
- A sample label adapted for the Uzbek market, with a field for the importer's details.
- A halal certificate, or a clear explanation of why the product does not need one.
- Shelf life at production and the residual shelf life you can guarantee on arrival.
- Storage and transport temperatures, and who holds the goods at each stage.
- A price basis with Incoterms, a minimum order quantity and a realistic lead time.

Chains focus on documents and residual shelf life, because both decide whether the distribution centre accepts the pallet. Wholesalers look at margin and payment terms, while HoReCa buyers ask about pack sizes, batch consistency and reorder speed.

## Conformity and labelling for food export to Uzbekistan

Imported food has to confirm conformity with national technical regulations, usually through a certificate or a declaration of conformity based on laboratory tests, depending on the product group. Products of animal origin also face sanitary and veterinary requirements. Rules change, so confirm the current requirements for your product codes with the national agency for technical regulation and standardisation and with a licensed customs broker before you print a single label.

Food labelling requirements in Uzbekistan start with language: consumer information must be given in the state language, Uzbek. Most packs on the shelf also carry Russian, and buyers often expect both. A compliant label typically includes the following.

- The product name and a description of what the product is.
- The full list of ingredients, with allergens clearly identified.
- Net quantity in metric units.
- Date of production, shelf life and storage conditions.
- The manufacturer's name and address, and those of the importer in Uzbekistan.
- Nutritional value and any conformity or halal marks the product is entitled to carry.

Some product groups also fall under mandatory digital marking with a scannable code, so ask your broker whether your category is in scope. The most common mistake is a sticker that hides the original label or peels off in a refrigerated truck; test it in real storage conditions and agree its placement with the importer.

## Halal certification for food sold in Uzbekistan

Most consumers in Uzbekistan are Muslim, and halal is a commercial expectation rather than a niche claim. For meat, poultry, sausages and ready meals a buyer will rarely list a product without a certificate. For confectionery, dairy and snacks the question shifts to ingredients such as gelatine, emulsifiers and flavourings.

A halal certificate is issued by a certification body after an audit of the plant, the raw materials and the process. Before you apply for one or rely on an existing certificate, check these points.

- Ask your importer which halal certification bodies local buyers recognise, and whether a certificate from your home country is accepted.
- Make sure the certificate names the production site and lists the products you plan to export.
- Check the validity date against your shipment calendar, so it does not expire while goods are in transit.
- Put the halal mark only on SKUs the certificate covers, and keep a copy in the shipment file.

## Shelf life and cold-chain logistics

Distance eats shelf life. Chains usually set a minimum share of remaining shelf life at acceptance, and a product that left the factory in good time can still be refused at the distribution centre. Ask each buyer for that rule early and work backwards: production, consolidation, transit, customs clearance, warehouse acceptance and delivery to stores.

For chilled and frozen products, plan the whole chain rather than the truck: refrigerated transport with temperature loggers, a named person who reads the logger data, and temperature-controlled storage and delivery at the importer. Test the route with a smaller first shipment and record actual transit days.

## Pricing that leaves room for the distributor

A home-market ex-works price often ends up as a shelf price nobody pays. Build the shelf price from the bottom up before the first meeting.

1. Start with your ex-works or FCA price per unit and per pallet.
2. Add freight, insurance and forwarding to the importer's warehouse.
3. Add customs duties, VAT and clearance costs, using current rates from your customs broker.
4. Add the importer's and distributor's margin, which pays for storage, delivery and credit to retailers.
5. Add the retailer's margin and the cost of listing, promotions and tastings.
6. Compare the result with the shelf price of competing products and adjust pack size or margin if the gap is too wide.

Leave headroom for at least one promotion in the first months, and agree in writing who pays for it.

### A first-shipment timeline in weeks

1. Before the show: check product codes, labelling and the conformity route with a customs broker.
2. Weeks 1–2 after the show: agree the importer, Incoterms and a trial order.
3. Weeks 3–8: obtain or confirm conformity documents and the halal certificate, and finalise labels.
4. Weeks 8–12: ship a trial volume, record real transit and acceptance times, then plan regular deliveries.

## FAQ

### Do I need a local importer to export food to Uzbekistan?

In most cases, yes. The importer appears on the label, clears the goods and often holds the conformity documents.

### Is a label in Russian enough?

No. Consumer information has to be in Uzbek, the state language, with Russian commonly added. Confirm current rules for your category with the technical regulation agency and your broker.

### Is halal certification mandatory?

Treat it as mandatory in practice for meat and poultry and as a strong advantage elsewhere. Check which certification bodies your buyer accepts before you pay for an audit.

### Can I bring samples to FOODERA EXPO for tastings?

Yes. Tastings require a product document pack, and the account manager sends a checklist with your application.
