### A delegation travels for a decision, not for impressions

Purchasing decisions get made where three people stand in front of the same stand: the one who **selects** (technologist, agronomist, chef, IT director), the one who **costs it out** (finance, procurement) and the one who **signs** (owner, director). Send only the engineer and you come back with leaflets and a three-month approval cycle. Send all three and you come back with a draft contract.

**Optimal size:** 3–5 people per company. More — the route dissolves; fewer — nobody is there to ask about price and terms.

### Write the goal before you leave

Not "look at the market", but three lines:

* **What we are looking for** — drip irrigation for 200 ha, two packaging SKUs, replacing a fuel supplier.
* **Who we talk to** — 6–10 companies picked from the exhibitor catalogue. Walking every aisle buys fatigue, not contacts.
* **What we bring back** — three offers, two price lists with deferral terms, one agreement to meet at your site.

Send that wording to the venue manager before the visit: on request we show free slots at the buyer matchmaking desk and help book meetings, so the group arrives for negotiations rather than for a crowd.

### Registration, badges, entry

Online trade registration issues a QR ticket; at the desk be ready to confirm your profile — company card, work e-mail or a letter. For a group it is easier to walk in with one list: name, role, company, phone.

Educational groups have their own logic: at WORLD EDU EXPO (9–10 April 2027) organized groups get a separate entrance, a guided tour of the exhibition and aptitude testing — so a class or a year group moves along a route with an escort instead of scattering across the hall.

### The logistics of the day

* **Road.** The centre sits in the Dzhambay district of Samarkand region: 16 km from the airport, 23 km from the railway station. For a delegation from Tashkent, Bukhara, Navoi or Karshi it is one bus leg, not a flight.
* **Parking.** A large car park on the grounds: the group minibus stops next to the entrance rather than queuing for street parking.
* **Food.** Two cafés, 350 seats in total: a group of thirty eats without a queue and is back in the hall before the day ends.
* **Connectivity.** Free Wi-Fi and LAN across the site — you can put the object on a video call and get the head-office approval on the spot.
* **Negotiations.** A conference room with an AV system: if the day ends with gathering three suppliers in one room, that happens at the venue, not in a hotel lobby.

### A route across sections, not along aisles

The exposition takes 4,400 m² indoors and 5,000 m² outdoors. A workable day is three 90-minute blocks:

1. **Morning, before the flow.** Target companies from the pre-built list: terms recorded, demos agreed.
2. **Midday, business programme.** Forums and sessions: this is where regulation, logistics and next-season pricing become audible.
3. **Late afternoon, loose ends.** Tastings, demo cycles outdoors, a second visit to whoever you could not detail with.

Between blocks the delegation compares notes. One person spotted a novelty, the second saw a delivery-date risk, the third knows this supplier already tried to sell to your competitor.

### The report: five fields, readable as a procurement plan

A format that does not turn into "it was interesting":

| Supplier | What they offer | Price and terms | Risk | Next step, date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Below it — three conclusions: what enters the pipeline, what is postponed, who we go back to for a second round. If the show produces no line in the "next step" column, the problem is usually not the exhibition but the brief written before departure.
