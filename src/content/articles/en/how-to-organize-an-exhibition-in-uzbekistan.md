To organize an exhibition in Uzbekistan, you need the same building blocks as anywhere else: a clear format, a realistic timeline, a budget with every line visible, a venue that fits the technical brief and an audience that has a reason to come. This guide is for associations, industry organisers and companies planning their own trade show, conference, congress or corporate event, in the order decisions have to be made.

## Define the format and the audience first

Every later decision depends on two answers: what kind of event this is, and who must be in the room for it to succeed.

- A trade show sells floor space to exhibitors and attracts professional visitors; its success is measured in contracts signed and exhibitors who return.
- A conference or congress sells seats and sponsorship; the programme and the speakers carry it, and the exhibition area is secondary.
- A corporate event serves one company's goals, such as a dealer meeting, a product launch or an employee expo, and has no ticket sales.
- A festival or public show needs outdoor space, a stage and crowd management more than stand grids.

Write down the exhibitor profile, the visitor profile and one measurable goal, such as the number of buyer meetings. If you cannot name the audience in one sentence, the marketing budget will be spent guessing.

## Timeline to organize an exhibition in Uzbekistan

A first edition needs about a year. Repeat events can move faster because the database and partners already exist.

1. 12–9 months out: confirm the concept, the budget envelope and the date; send briefs to venues and lock the date with a contract, since good dates in spring and autumn are booked early.
2. 6 months out: publish the event page and floor plan, open stand sales, sign key partners and the general build contractor, and start the speaker list.
3. 3 months out: collect exhibitor technical forms for power, stand height and custom builds, agree the safety and build regulations with the venue, and launch visitor marketing.
4. 1 month out: open visitor registration, send invitations and press accreditation, finalise the timed programme, and order catering, transport and staff.
5. Show week: run the truck schedule and build-up, brief contractors, test registration, wayfinding and connectivity, and hold a daily briefing in one operations room.
6. After the show: dismantle, close the acts and invoices, and prepare the report within a few weeks while contacts are still warm.

## Budget lines to plan before you sign

There is no universal price for an event; a venue rate depends on season, format, build density and calendar load. What you can control is completeness. A realistic budget lists at least these lines.

- Venue: space rental per day, including build-up and dismantle days, which are often priced differently from show days.
- Build: shell scheme or custom stands, carpets, furniture, branding and the organiser's own areas such as registration and the press zone.
- Technical: power connections, lighting, sound, screens, internet, interpretation and streaming.
- Marketing: website, advertising, mailings, printed materials, press work and the cost of bringing hosted buyers.
- Staff: project team, registration, hosts, security and cleaning where they are not included in the venue package.
- Logistics: freight, unloading, storage, transfers and accommodation for speakers and guests.
- Insurance and permits: liability insurance and any approvals required for the format.
- Contingency: a reserve for late changes, currency movement and services that exhibitors order on site.

Ask every venue for a quote that follows the same structure, otherwise you will compare incomparable figures.

## Choosing an exhibition venue in Samarkand: a checklist

Whether you look at Tashkent, Samarkand or another city, test each venue against one list.

- Space: indoor and outdoor area, column grid, and whether the hall and outdoor zone can run together.
- Floor load and ceiling height: request them in writing for heavy machinery and tall custom stands.
- Power: installed capacity, voltage, distribution to stands and separate supply for demonstrations.
- Access: truck routes to the unloading zone, doors, cranes and the build-up window.
- Parking: space for visitor cars, coaches and contractor vehicles.
- Wi-Fi and LAN: site-wide coverage and wired connections for exhibitors and streaming.
- Catering: on-site cafés, coffee-break options and outside catering rules.
- Conference venue: seats, projection, interpretation booths and recording if the event has a programme.

## Permits, safety and coordination with authorities

Requirements depend on the format, the site and the products on display, so treat this as a list of questions to raise early with the venue and the local authorities, not as legal advice.

- Fire safety: stand materials and the overall fire load are approved with the venue's safety officer, and evacuation plans are posted at entrances.
- Special activities: open flame, cooking, live animals or public performances of music usually need separate approvals.
- Foreign exhibitors: samples crossing the border need customs paperwork, so connect exhibitors with a customs broker in advance.
- Public space: if the event affects streets or traffic, the city administration needs to be informed in good time.

Confirm in writing who is responsible for each approval.

## Selling stands, filling the hall and reporting afterwards

### Selling stands

Publish a floor plan with clear packages and a deadline for early booking. Exhibitors buy an audience, so show them who will attend, which buyers are invited and what the business programme offers.

### Attracting visitors

Combine registration with a reason to come: a forum, buyer matchmaking, product contests or demonstrations. Industry associations, regional administrations and partner media help reach professionals.

### The post-event report

Send exhibitors and partners a report with attendance, visitor profile, programme activity, feedback and logistics notes. Add recommendations and a date for the next edition, because rebooking starts from this document.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to organize an exhibition in Uzbekistan?

A first edition needs about 9–12 months; a repeat event or a conference can be prepared in less if the date and partners are fixed early.

### What should be in the brief to a venue?

Format, preferred dates, area, expected exhibitors and visitors, and technical requirements such as power, heavy exhibits or stage needs.

### Can a third-party organiser rent the SOF EXPO venue?

Yes, the 4 400 m² hall, the 5 000 m² open area and the 350-seat conference hall are available for exhibitions, forums, festivals and corporate events.

### Who handles permits and safety approvals?

Responsibility is shared between the organiser and the venue; agree it in the contract and confirm current requirements with the relevant local authorities.
