### Why trade shows still work

B2B decisions are made on trust, not clicks. An exhibition shortens the sales cycle because in one day a buyer can hold the product, watch it work, ask an uncomfortable question and see who else is on the market. No digital channel delivers that density of context.

#### What to measure

| Metric | How to count | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Qualified contacts | share of leads with decision role and budget | not leaflets, but decision makers |
| Cost per contact | show budget / qualified leads | comparable with digital CPL |
| Pipeline | value of opportunities created within 90 days | the metric for B2B |
| Payback | margin from new contracts / budget | measure quarterly, not weekly |
| Audience | organizer report: visitors, geography, roles | cross-check with your own counter |

#### Planning that produces a result

1. **A numeric goal.** "Five dealers" beats "raise awareness".
2. **Invitations before the doors open.** Meetings confirmed in advance produce most of the contracts: by the time you arrive, the buyer has already reserved the slot.
3. **The right team.** One person closes, another collects contacts — not the same person doing both.
4. **Materials with data.** Price, specification and a case in one folder; without them the talk ends in "we will think".
5. **48-hour follow-up.** A contact handled on day two converts roughly twice as well as one handled after a week.

#### Common mistakes

* exhibiting "because competitors do";
* saving on lighting — a dark stand loses half its traffic;
* agreeing no demo scenario with the venue engineers;
* sending staff without decision authority;
* ignoring the organizer report and arguing with numbers later.
