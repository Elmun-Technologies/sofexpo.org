Selling on marketplaces in Uzbekistan is the fastest way for a brand to reach buyers across the country without opening a single store. The platforms, however, reward only sellers who arrive with a proper legal setup, clean product cards and numbers that still add up after commissions. This guide covers onboarding, assortment, cards in Uzbek and Russian, pricing, fulfilment, reviews and ads, and ends with a unit economics checklist to fill in before the first stock leaves your warehouse.

## Where to start: platforms and seller onboarding

E-commerce in Uzbekistan is built around a handful of platforms. Uzum Market is the largest local marketplace and usually the first one sellers evaluate, while Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex Market also operate in the country. Each has its own seller portal, category rules, commissions and logistics options, and all of them change regularly, so check current terms in each seller portal before you model prices.

Onboarding follows a similar path on most platforms. Expect to prepare the following.

- A registered legal entity or individual entrepreneur in Uzbekistan; foreign brands usually work through a local company or distributor unless the platform runs a cross-border programme.
- A bank account and tax registration that match the entity named in the seller agreement.
- Conformity documents for regulated categories such as food, cosmetics, children's goods and electronics.
- Proof of rights to the brand, such as a trademark registration or a distribution agreement with the brand owner.
- A contact person who can answer moderators and customers in Uzbek or Russian.

Ask a local accountant how marketplace payouts are taxed for your entity type before you sign, because the answer goes straight into your price.

## Assortment and product cards in Uzbek and Russian

Start narrow. A focused range you can keep in stock beats a long list that sells out after the first promotion. Choose products with clear search demand, sensible size and weight for delivery, and a low risk of damage or return.

A product card is your shelf and your shop assistant in one. Buyers search in both Uzbek and Russian, and Uzbek text is usually written in Latin script, so a card in one language misses part of the audience. Write each card natively in both languages rather than machine-translating it, and use the words people actually type.

### Photos that sell

- A main photo on a clean background that shows the product itself, not just the box.
- Several photos of the product in use, at real scale, showing the details buyers ask about.
- An infographic with the key specification in both languages, readable on a phone screen.
- Clear photos of the label and composition for food, cosmetics and children's goods.

## Pricing, commissions and fulfilment models

On a marketplace, price is built from the shelf down, not from the cost up. The platform takes a commission that depends on the category, charges for logistics, storage and sometimes returns, and runs sales events in which sellers are expected to discount. Because all of these change, rebuild your price every time the platform publishes new terms.

### Marketplace warehouse or your own

Most platforms offer two main models. In the first, you deliver stock to the marketplace warehouse and the platform stores, packs and delivers; you pay for logistics and storage, but delivery is faster and daily work is lighter. In the second, you keep stock in your own warehouse and hand over each order, which gives you control but makes delivery speed and accuracy your responsibility. Many sellers start from their own warehouse to test demand, then move fast-selling SKUs to the marketplace warehouse.

## Reviews, ads and the first 12 weeks

New cards start with no reviews and no sales history, so the first weeks decide how visible they become. Plan the launch as a sequence rather than waiting for organic traffic.

1. Weeks 1–2: publish the cards, ship limited stock and check that search, prices and delivery estimates display correctly in both languages.
2. Weeks 3–6: run the platform's internal ads on a fixed daily budget and track which search terms bring orders, not only clicks.
3. Weeks 3–8: answer every review and question within a day, because a polite, specific reply to a negative review is read by the next buyers.
4. Weeks 6–12: cut SKUs that do not sell even with ads, restock the winners and move them to faster fulfilment if the numbers allow.

Never buy fake reviews: platforms penalise it, and buyers spot it quickly.

## Unit economics checklist for selling on marketplaces in Uzbekistan

Fill in these lines for every SKU before you ship stock. If the result is negative at the planned price, the marketplace will only scale the loss.

- Purchase or production cost per unit, including packaging and labelling.
- Delivery to the marketplace warehouse or to your own warehouse.
- The marketplace commission for the category, taken from the current seller portal.
- Logistics, storage and return fees under the fulfilment model you chose.
- Advertising cost per order, estimated from the first weeks of test campaigns.
- The planned promotional discount and how often you will join platform sales.
- Taxes and payment fees for your entity type.
- Stock tied up in the warehouse and how many weeks it takes to turn.
- Margin per unit and per month after all of the above.

## FAQ

### Can a foreign company sell on Uzum Market?

Usually through a local legal entity or a distributor that holds the seller account. Check the current requirements for non-resident sellers in the Uzum Market seller portal, as the rules can change.

### Which marketplace should I start with?

Start where your category already sells and where you can meet the logistics requirements. Many brands launch on one platform, stabilise stock and reviews, and then add a second.

### Is Wildberries in Uzbekistan worth adding alongside a local platform?

It can be, especially for brands that already sell on it in other countries. Compare commissions, logistics and delivery times for your category in each seller portal before deciding.

### Do I need product cards in both Uzbek and Russian?

Yes, if you want to reach the whole market. Buyers search in both languages, and a card written natively in each reads as more trustworthy than a machine translation.
