Uzbekistan market entry usually fails for a simple reason: a manufacturer commits to an office, a warehouse or an exclusive partner before checking who actually buys the product and at what price. A trade show reverses that order. In three days you see real buyers, real competitors and real distributors in one hall, and only then decide how much to invest. This guide is a step-by-step plan for exporters from Türkiye, China, Russia, Kazakhstan and the EU who want to test the market before they commit.

## Uzbekistan market entry: why start with a trade show

Doing business in Uzbekistan is relationship-driven. Distributors and chain buyers want to see the product, the people behind it and the documents before they talk about volumes. Remote prospecting by e-mail and video calls produces polite interest but rarely a first order.

Trade shows in Uzbekistan give you three things that are expensive to get any other way:

- A demand test: which items people pick up, ask about and want to price, and which ones they walk past.
- A shortlist of partners: distributors, dealers and importers who come to the hall precisely to find new suppliers.
- A price reality check: what competitors already sell on the local market, in which packaging and on what terms.

The cost is a stand, samples and a team trip. Compared with opening a legal entity or signing an exclusive contract blind, it is the cheapest way to learn whether export to Uzbekistan makes sense for your product.

## The 12-week plan before the show

Treat the show as a project with a single measurable goal, for example "five qualified distributor meetings and two sample orders". Then work backwards.

### Weeks 12 to 9: decide what you are testing

1. Choose three to five products for the test, not the whole catalogue.
2. Set an indicative export price range and the minimum order you can accept.
3. Check with a customs broker whether your products need certification or registration before they can be sold.
4. Book the stand and ask the organiser about matchmaking and the business programme.

### Weeks 8 to 5: prepare the materials

1. Translate a short catalogue and price list into Russian, and add Uzbek where your buyers expect it.
2. Prepare a one-page partner offer: territory, margins, marketing support, payment terms.
3. Arrange samples and their shipping with a freight forwarder, leaving a buffer for customs clearance.
4. Make a list of target distributors and invite them to the stand in advance.

### Weeks 4 to 1: fix the meetings

1. Confirm meeting times with the people on your list and the matchmaking schedule.
2. Brief the stand team: who qualifies leads, who talks prices, who signs.
3. Prepare a simple lead form with the same questions for every visitor.
4. Book travel to Samarkand and plan one extra day for warehouse or office visits.

## Distributor, dealer or own office: choosing the partner model

The show is where you collect the evidence for this decision. Each model fits a different situation.

### Distributor

A distributor buys your goods, holds stock, handles import formalities and sells to retail or trade. This suits fast-moving goods and companies that want low fixed costs. The trade-off is less control over price and shelf presence, so agree on reporting and minimum volumes.

### Dealer network

Dealers are better for machinery, equipment and building systems where service, spare parts and installation matter. Several regional dealers give wider coverage than one national distributor, but you need to train them and support warranty claims.

### Own office or representative

An own office makes sense once volumes are proven and you need direct control over key accounts, pricing and after-sales service. It is the most expensive option and the hardest to reverse, so it rarely belongs at the start.

A practical rule: start with a distributor or a small group of dealers on a non-exclusive trial, and grant exclusivity only after agreed volume targets are met.

## The 30 days after the show

Most of the value of a show is lost in the first month. Plan the follow-up before you travel.

1. Days 1 to 3: send every qualified contact a short summary of what was discussed and the agreed next step.
2. Days 4 to 10: send price lists, partner terms and sample offers to the shortlist, and score each candidate on reach, warehouse and finances.
3. Days 11 to 20: hold second meetings online or in person, ask for references and check the partner's existing brands.
4. Days 21 to 30: agree a trial order or a pilot territory with clear volumes, payment terms and a review date.

At the end of the month you should know which model you are choosing and why. If no candidate passed your criteria, that is also a result: you learned it for the price of a stand.

## Common mistakes

- Bringing the whole catalogue instead of a focused test range.
- Granting exclusivity to the first distributor who shows interest.
- Quoting prices without checking import duties and certification costs.
- Leaving follow-up until the team has recovered from the trip.

## FAQ

### Do I need a local company to sell in Uzbekistan?

Not at the start. Many exporters sell through a local distributor or importer who handles import and resale. Confirm the current rules for your product with a customs broker or legal adviser before signing.

### Which language should my materials be in?

Russian is widely used in business, and Uzbek is the state language. A Russian catalogue with key information in Uzbek covers most trade buyers; English works for some importers.

### How many partners should I meet at one show?

Aim for quality over volume. Five to ten qualified distributor or dealer conversations are usually enough to compare options and pick a trial partner.

### Can I test the market without exhibiting?

Yes. Visiting a show as a buyer lets you study competitors and meet distributors. Exhibiting gives you more inbound contacts and lets buyers see the product in person.
